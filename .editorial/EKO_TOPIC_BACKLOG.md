# EKO Perspectives topic backlog

**Planning document. Updated 1 October 2026.**

This file captures promising post directions from the current editorial review. It is not a publication schedule and none of these topics should be drafted or published without the normal weekly planning and owner approval step.

Any factual premise below must be re-checked against current primary sources before drafting. Ideas tied to unpublished or under-review research must wait until public release or explicit clearance.

## Priority ideas

### 1. Ireland's tree-planting promise, year by year

**Engine:** claim test / audit.

Compare afforestation targets in successive national forest strategies and climate plans with actual planting from official annual statistics. Then examine the most evidence-supported reasons for the gap, such as licensing, land prices, farmer participation and the permanence of land-use change.

**Editorial aim:** one chart, one clearly defined promise, one evidence-based verdict.

### 2. One Irish number

**Engine:** recurring data commentary.

Short posts built around one figure from a scheduled public release such as Teagasc National Farm Survey, CSO agricultural statistics, EPA inventories or forest statistics.

Possible questions:
- What does the gap between dairy and drystock incomes imply for a national livestock target?
- What does one year's afforestation number mean against the official target?
- What changed in agricultural emissions and which component drove it?

**Length guide:** roughly 300 to 500 words when the evidence permits.

### 3. The same acre: Leitrim and the competing claims on marginal land

**Engine:** place-led public-record piece, with a real scene only if visited.

Use Dáil records, county plans, forestry studies, consultation submissions and farm-sector reporting to show how a national forestry or land-use target meets local incidence.

Close on what spatial modelling can illuminate and what it cannot settle in a dispute involving livelihoods, landscape, ownership and national targets.

### 4. Who owns the trees? Tenure under Ghana's cocoa farms

**Engine:** institution underneath.

Examine how tree tenure and ownership rules affect farmers' incentives to retain or remove shade trees. Verify the current legal and reform position before drafting.

Use Forestry Commission material, legislation and peer-reviewed evidence. Do not invent observations from Ghana; use only documented sources or genuine first-hand memories.

### 5. Why biofuel accounting kept changing its mind

**Engine:** debate diagnosis / intellectual history.

Trace how biofuel accounting changed as researchers incorporated indirect land-use change, spatial data and different counterfactuals. Use a small set of pivotal studies plus policy responses such as the Renewable Fuel Standard and Sodsaver.

**Constraint:** public background only. Do not reveal unpublished results from the user's own corn-ethanol work.

### 6. The landowner isn't always the farmer

**Engine:** institution underneath, U.S. edition.

Explain why voluntary biomass or conservation programmes may need to persuade landowners as well as farm operators. Use current USDA tenure evidence and Census of Agriculture tables rather than remembered statistics.

This can explain the conceptual logic behind BioLand-US without disclosing results.

### 7. Hardin, Ostrom and the commonage

**Engine:** reading pair applied to a real institution.

Set Hardin's argument against Ostrom's evidence, then examine Irish commonage through official agri-environment scheme documents and farm-organisation submissions.

The piece should decide which framework better describes the institution and explain why.

### 8. Two futures for the uplands

**Engine:** two-school debate diagnosis.

Present the strongest evidence for rewilding and hill-farming positions, identify what both sides agree on, and isolate the empirical questions that would genuinely change the conclusion.

Take a position only if the evidence supports one.

### 9. What China's Grain for Green can teach a voluntary scheme

**Engine:** comparative policy lesson.

Explain the programme and evaluation evidence, then ask what, if anything, transfers to Irish voluntary scheme design.

Keep institutional and historical differences explicit. Living in China is relevant context but does not by itself supply evidence about the programme.

### 10. Will Africa's yields grow fast enough?

**Engine:** own work explained.

**Status:** wait until the relevant paper is public and co-author / journal communication constraints are cleared.

When public, explain why persistence matters alongside yield growth, how the 2050 benchmark is constructed, and what the study shows in plain language with one strong figure.

### 11. Auditing a national promise: BECCS in Ireland

**Engine:** own work explained / feasibility audit.

**Status:** results-based version waits for publication.

Before then, a general public-source explainer could examine what any BECCS commitment depends on: land, biomass supply, conversion, storage and permanence.

### 12. What I learned from talking to data

**Engine:** personal essay.

Develop the existing line about learning when to stop talking long enough to let the data answer back. Use genuine episodes from teaching, helping colleagues, China and Galway.

The piece should distinguish running a model from understanding an answer.

## Standing planning rule

Do not fill a calendar merely to maintain output. Before drafting any item, confirm:
1. the angle is still current and worth saying;
2. the evidence is available and public;
3. the piece does not duplicate a recent post;
4. the chosen engine fits the material;
5. the owner has approved the topic and angle.
