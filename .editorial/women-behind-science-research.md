# Women Behind the Science: research and image ledger

**Status:** Editorial research is complete enough for drafting the first twenty profiles. All twenty profile pages exist as `draft: true`. Publication remains blocked by image-rights clearance, final fact review, rendered desktop/phone checks and owner approval.

## Editorial rules

- Profiles use the `people/places behind the science` engine: person + question + place + people + method + consequence.
- Current roles are checked against institutional pages as of 6 October 2026.
- Institutional biographies are treated as starting points, not sole evidence.
- “First publication” is avoided unless a comprehensive bibliographic check establishes it. Profiles use “early publication anchor” where appropriate.
- Publication counts are omitted from prose when databases and institutional pages use different coverage.
- Portraits must be stored locally and must have a reuse basis that satisfies the site image policy. Institutional hosting alone is not permission.
- A strong image should preferably show researcher + object/place/collaborators. A clean portrait is the fallback, not the default ideal.

## Research anchors: profiles 1–10

### Jill Farrant
Primary institutional anchors: UCT Plant Stress Lab; UCT 2025 NRF Lifetime Achievement profile. Early anchor: 1989 *Planta* work on recalcitrant-seed desiccation sensitivity. Current research: multi-omics, resurrection grasses and *Myrothamnus* genome work.

### Dilys Sefakor MacCarthy
Primary anchors: University of Ghana SIREC profile and SIREC institutional history. Important current-title caution: public UG pages have used both “former Head” and “Head”; opening copy therefore uses only the verified professorial role. Early anchor: 2009 APSIM nutrient/residue-management work.

### Anne Mullen
Primary anchor: University of Galway research profile. Career chronology: LSHTM/KCL research and teaching, 2005–2014; Dairy Council, 2014–2018; *Nature Food* launch Chief Editor, 2019–2023; Galway from January 2023.

### Rachel Bezner Kerr
Primary anchor: Cornell CALS profile. Independent evidence: peer-reviewed publications from long-running Malawi work. Early anchor located: 2005 *Journal of Southern African Studies*. Do not describe this as definitively her first publication.

### Jessica Duncan
Primary anchors: Wageningen staff profile and research portal. Evidence includes the 2015 CFS governance book, 2018 participation paper, current PhD supervision and 2017 Teacher of the Year record.

### Radhika Khosla
Primary anchors: Oxford ZERO Institute, Somerville College and 2026 professorship announcement. Correction preserved: PhD is in Geophysical Sciences from the University of Chicago; undergraduate and master's training are in Physics at Oxford.

### Sinéad Waters
Primary anchor: University of Galway research profile. Current role is Lecturer/PI, not Professor. Career includes Teagasc 2005–January 2024 and Galway from 2024. Teaching and research are unusually well documented.

### Irene S. Egyir
Primary anchor: University of Ghana Department of Agricultural Economics and Agribusiness. Department correction preserved. Official profile records 29 years of experience and around 40 publications.

### Maura Farrell
Primary anchor: University of Galway research profile. Avoid unverified claims about a “first publication”. The stronger chronology is the evolution from agricultural change and rural development into gender, spatial justice, participation and FLIARA.

### Martine Visser
Primary anchors: UCT EPRU and RUBEN. The Cape Town water study involved a randomised trial of about 400,000 households. Current work broadens from nudges to governance and financing of ecological infrastructure.

## Research anchors: profiles 11–20

### Freda Elikplim Asem
Primary anchor: University of Ghana Department of Agricultural Economics and Agribusiness. Current role is Associate Professor, not Senior Lecturer. UG records more than 20 years in agriculture, a PhD in Development Studies, an MSc in Agricultural and Resource Economics and leadership of the West Africa hub of the Feed the Future Innovation Lab for Horticulture. Current 2026 work includes credit and farmer welfare, crop diversification and indigenous food markets. Avoid unstable publication-count claims.

### Monica Lambon-Quayefio
Primary anchor: University of Ghana Department of Economics. Current page labels her Associate Professor on sabbatical leave, although its appointment history still records Senior Lecturer from 2019. PhD Economics, Clark University, 2014. 2026 *Journal of Development Economics* experiment verified: digital credit increased investment but produced few statistically significant average downstream effects; timing of loan delivery was an important implementation complication.

### Jacqueline Naalamle Amissah
Primary anchors: University of Ghana Pure and research intelligence systems. Current UG research directory identifies her as Associate Professor in Crop Science. Research activity is documented from 2008 onward. Career focus is plant propagation, horticulture, tissue culture, domestication and indigenous/underused species. Do not use database work counts as a biographical claim.

### Pearl Abu
Primary anchors: WACCI/University of Ghana and UNESCO. Correct recognition date is 2025, not 2024. UNESCO identifies her as one of the first laureates of the fellowship for young women scientists in Africa and highlights protein/provitamin-A enriched stress-tolerant maize and Ghana's first doubled-haploid maize platform. The core story is trait stacking plus the adoption gap.

### Rachel Wynberg
Primary anchors: UCT Environmental & Geographical Science, ACDI and IPBES. Holds the SARChI Chair in Environmental and Social Dimensions of the Bio-economy. UCT confirms supervision of PhD, Masters and Honours students and teaching of Critical Perspectives on the Bio-economy. IPBES confirms her Sustainable Use Assessment role. Profile should centre governance, benefit sharing and competing visions of the bio-economy rather than publication totals.

### Katye Altieri
Primary anchor: UCT Oceanography. Current title Associate Professor. Research spans atmospheric chemistry, marine aerosols, atmosphere-ocean exchange and marine biogeochemistry. Southern Ocean fieldwork provides the physical setting. The 2025 Oppenheimer New Frontiers Research Award is verified. Avoid claiming that the remote Southern Ocean is literally pristine; the profile treats preindustrial analogy as a hypothesis that must be tested.

### Gina Ziervogel
Primary anchors: UCT ACDI and Environmental & Geographical Science. Current role Professor and Director of ACDI. PhD Geography, Oxford, 2002. UCT confirms approximately 25 years in adaptation/vulnerability research and lead-author role on IPCC AR6 Cities, Settlements and Key Infrastructure. Her August 2026 inaugural lecture provides a current anchor on governing adaptation trade-offs.

### Petra Holden
Primary anchor: UCT ACDI / ARUA. Current role Senior Researcher and lead of the People in Nature & Climate Lab. Current programme combines field studies, modelling, AI and synthesis around nature-based solutions. Verified 2024 *Conservation Biology* work addresses methodological pluralism in counterfactuals. Current profile also records 2023 Frontiers Planet Prize and AXA XL Climate Water Nexus Award.

### Anna Steynor
Primary anchors: UK Met Office and former UCT CSAG record. Major correction: she is no longer Head of Climate Services at UCT. The Met Office currently lists her as Senior Scientist in international climate services in the Climate Services Development team. The story should retain her long UCT climate-services trajectory but use the Met Office as current affiliation.

### Britta Rennkamp
Primary anchor: UCT ACDI. Current role Senior Researcher; co-directs the ARUA Centre of Excellence in Climate and Development and leads climate-governance work. Training includes PhD in Science Technology Policy Studies, University of Twente, and interdisciplinary Latin American regional studies at Cologne. Correct publication anchor: the widely relevant *Climate Policy* paper on power, coalitions and institutional change was published online in 2019, not 2025.
