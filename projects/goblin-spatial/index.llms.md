# GOBLIN-Spatial

Where national agricultural and land-use pathways fall across Ireland, and whether the resulting spatial transition is feasible.

## The question

National AFOLU pathways can specify how livestock numbers, land release and future land uses change without showing **where those changes fall**.

GOBLIN-Spatial asks:

> **When a nationally coherent agricultural transition requires restructuring, where does transition pressure concentrate, what can the released land support, and can competing future land-use requirements be accommodated within the available geography?**

GOBLIN supplies the national pathway. GOBLIN-Spatial resolves its geography and tests spatial feasibility.

## How it works

``` text
National GOBLIN pathway
        ↓
Transition incidence
Where does adjustment fall?
        ↓
Spatial response potential
What can released land support?
        ↓
Spatial transformability
Can competing future land uses fit?
        ↓
Spatial flexibility
Is one geography necessary?
```

The framework is a spatial stress-test of transition pathways, not a prediction of individual farmer or parcel decisions.

## Evidence and scale

The historical reconstruction covers 2015–2025 and is anchored to the 2020 Census of Agriculture at Electoral Division level. Higher-level livestock controls maintain national accounting consistency while preserving validated local structure.

The model combines agricultural statistics with land, soil and drainage information, LPIS context and explicit eligibility controls.

## What it measures

GOBLIN-Spatial can identify:

- where livestock adjustment and production exposure are concentrated;
- where land is released under a national pathway;
- what physical and agricultural opportunities exist within that transition space;
- whether competing future land-use requirements can fit within finite eligible land; and
- whether a feasible outcome depends on a narrow geography or can be delivered through alternatives.

A central distinction is maintained throughout:

> **Land released is not automatically land eligible, available, allocated or adopted.**

## What it does not claim

The model does not predict individual farmer behaviour, household welfare, market prices, parcel-level adoption or the exact geography that will exist in 2050.

Those questions require behavioural, farm-level or market models.
