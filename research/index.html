Research

Agricultural, land-use, bioenergy and policy modelling across national, spatial and farm scales.

My research focuses on ex-ante modelling of agricultural, land-use, bioenergy and environmental policy. I am interested in what happens between a policy target and its eventual implementation: how technical assumptions translate into changes in production, land use and behaviour, how those changes are distributed across places and producers, and what that means for policy design.

A central theme is that national objectives are implemented through heterogeneous production systems, land resources, farms and places. Aggregate feasibility therefore does not guarantee local feasibility, economic accessibility or behavioural participation.

Research questions

Much of my work follows a common sequence:

Policy objective or proposed intervention
        ↓
Technical and economic assumptions
        ↓
Production-system response
        ↓
Spatial and regional incidence
        ↓
Economic and behavioural response
        ↓
Opportunities, constraints and uncertainty
        ↓
Policy design, targeting and implementation

Questions include:

  • How can national agricultural, land-use and bioenergy objectives be translated into realistic spatial and economic pathways?
  • Where do transition costs, opportunities and implementation constraints concentrate?
  • Which technically available resources remain economically accessible and realistically deployable?
  • How do alternative incentives, compensation rules and allocation principles change who bears adjustment?
  • How can national pathway models be connected to local geography and farm-level heterogeneity?
  • What can be learned before major policy and investment decisions become difficult to reverse?

Agricultural and land-use transitions

This work examines AFOLU mitigation, livestock restructuring, afforestation, wetland rehabilitation, anaerobic digestion, bioeconomy pathways, land-use competition and long-term transition planning.

Rather than treating land release and land opportunity as equivalent, the modelling separates transition-space release, physical resource, eligibility, opportunity, allocation and actual adoption.

Spatial and distributional modelling

I use spatial reconstruction, local-area allocation, microsimulation and synthetic populations to investigate how nationally coherent changes are distributed across geography.

This includes transition exposure, land capability, released-land opportunity, regional heterogeneity and place-sensitive policy analysis.

Behavioural and policy-response modelling

A second strand examines producer response to policy instruments, including participation, stocking-rate adjustment, opportunity costs, compensation design, additionality and heterogeneous responses to alternative incentives.

The aim is to distinguish technical potential from economic accessibility and actual willingness to participate.

Bioenergy and resource mobilisation

My bioenergy work examines how resource estimates change when land competition, economic opportunity costs, contractual access and heterogeneous participation are introduced.

This includes Irish biomethane and land-use pathways as well as U.S. biomass, biofuel and land-access applications.

Food, climate and development

I also maintain a broader interest in food-system sustainability, agricultural productivity, carbon pricing, sustainable diets and climate-development trade-offs, including questions relevant to Sub-Saharan African food systems.

Methods

My work draws on:

  • Econometrics: panel models, conditional logit, correlated random effects and revealed-preference methods;
  • Behavioural and microsimulation modelling: producer response, farm-level simulation and policy-incidence analysis;
  • Optimisation and synthetic populations: mixed-integer optimisation, constraint-preserving allocation and synthetic farm construction;
  • Scenario and economic modelling: AFOLU pathways, marginal abatement costs, opportunity costs, bioenergy economics and transition stress-testing;
  • Spatial modelling: hierarchical reconstruction, spatial allocation, land capability and resource accessibility;
  • Programming and data: Python, R, Stata, GIS, SQL, Excel-based modelling and reproducible pipelines;
  • Uncertainty and validation: sensitivity analysis, scenario comparison, explicit assumptions, accounting closure and model diagnostics.

Reusable modelling systems

The longer-term objective is to build reusable modelling and decision-support systems, not just one-off analyses. Where possible, projects are structured so that assumptions, data requirements, scenario controls, code, tests and outputs can be inspected and extended by other researchers and analysts.
