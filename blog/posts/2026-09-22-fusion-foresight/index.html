Why I’m Excited to Work with FUSION and FORESIGHT

Research
FUSION
FORESIGHT
Land Use
A personal reflection on the modelling environment shaping my work on agriculture, land use and climate-neutral transitions.
Published

September 22, 2026

One of the most exciting parts of my PhD journey has been working in the FUSION research environment at the University of Galway and contributing to research connected with FORESIGHT.

FUSION stands for FUture Sustainable economy Integrated mOdelling Network. What attracts me to the group is that it does not treat sustainability as a problem that belongs to one discipline. Its work brings together life-cycle assessment, integrated assessment modelling, land-use analysis and environmental software engineering. The common thread is the use of quantitative models to ask practical questions about how economies, food systems and landscapes could change in the future.

That way of thinking fits naturally with the questions that interest me.

Agriculture and land use are full of difficult trade-offs. A national climate target can look clear on paper, but implementation happens through real production systems, farms, forests and communities. A pathway that appears technically feasible at national scale may look very different once land availability, livestock systems, economic incentives, spatial variation and alternative uses of land are considered.

This is where FORESIGHT, or Future ORiEnted ScenarIo modellinG of Holistic land use sTrategies, becomes especially interesting. The project provides agriculture and land-use modelling evidence for Ireland and continues the development of integrated AFOLU modelling tools such as GOBLIN. Its emphasis is not simply on predicting one future. It is about exploring plausible pathways, testing assumptions and understanding what different choices could mean for emissions, land use, resilience and wider sustainability objectives.

For me, that distinction matters. Forecasting asks what is likely to happen. Foresight asks a broader question: what futures are possible, what would be required to reach them, and what might we learn by working backwards from those outcomes?

My own work increasingly sits in that space. I am interested in taking national agricultural and land-use pathways and resolving them into more detailed spatial and farm-level questions. Where does adjustment occur? Which production systems are most exposed? What alternatives are actually feasible in particular places? How do economic and behavioural responses change the picture? And how can models become reusable decision-support tools rather than one-off academic exercises?

Working alongside researchers developing GOBLIN and related modelling approaches has reinforced something I increasingly value in research: the model itself is not the final product. The useful output is better evidence for a difficult decision.

That is what makes FUSION and FORESIGHT exciting to me. They create a space where environmental science, economics, modelling and software can meet around real policy problems, while keeping the analytical work transparent and open to improvement.

I expect many posts on this site will grow from that environment. Some will explain models and methods. Others will discuss new papers, data, land-use questions, agriculture, bioenergy or interesting developments in Ireland, the United States and elsewhere.

The aim is simple: use the blog as a place to think in public, connect research to current questions, and make technical work easier to understand.
