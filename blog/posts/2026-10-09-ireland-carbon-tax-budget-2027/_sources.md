# Public source ledger

This ledger records public evidence used for the published article. It contains no confidential or unpublished research material.

- Houses of the Oireachtas, Dáil Éireann, 7 October 2026. Budget 2027 debate. Supports the reduction of carbon tax on kerosene and natural gas, the scheduled €78.50 rate, maintenance of the lower rate for the lifetime of the Government, Fuel Allowance context, and Government's stated rationale: https://prelive.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/debate/dail/2026-10-07/20/
- National Enterprise Hub, Budget 2027 key changes. Supports the €48.50 per tonne rate: https://www.neh.gov.ie/the-source/budget-2027-key-changes
- Irish Times, 6 October 2026. Supports the immediate home-heating-oil saving estimate and the reversal to the 2023 rate: https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/10/06/big-policy-change-sees-carbon-tax-reversed-to-ease-home-heating-fuel-costs/
- Irish Times, 7 October 2026. Supports the reported Exchequer cost of €25 million in 2026 and €177 million in a full year, and quotes the Tax Strategy Group on the long-term signal: https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/10/07/carbon-tax-u-turn-calls-governments-climate-commitments-into-question/
- Houses of the Oireachtas, 17 June 2026. Supports more than €4.2 billion of carbon-tax revenue allocated since 2020 and the Government's pre-Budget statement that pausing the trajectory would reduce funding available for just-transition and climate measures: https://data.oireachtas.ie/ie/oireachtas/debateRecord/dail/2026-06-17/debate/mul%40/main.pdf
- ESRI, 9 October 2026. Supports the assessment that the home-heating carbon-tax cut is untargeted and expensive and reduces the decarbonisation incentive, and that targeted support could protect vulnerable households while preserving the incentive: https://www.esri.ie/news/budget-2027-tax-and-welfare-measures-keep-pace-with-forecast-inflation-for-2027
- EPA, May 2026 projections. Supports the 25% national reduction projection against a 51% target, agriculture up to 19%, transport up to 28%, and residential up to 18%: https://www.epa.ie/news-releases/news-releases-2026/epa-projections-show-ireland-remains-off-track-for-2030-climate-.php
