## Model piece (workflow Stage 4, 1990 to 2015 rule)

- **Model:** Alan Matthews, "A triumph for the Irish Presidency – a damp squib for CAP reform", CAP Reform blog (capreform.eu), 27 June 2013.
- **Its structure, which this post borrows:**
  1. News lede: what was agreed under the Irish presidency, by whom, and the parallel budget (MFF) deal.
  2. Frames presidency success separately from policy outcome.
  3. "Deal not yet complete": the issues farmers care about most (envelopes, transfers, capping) sit in the MFF talks, not the CAP talks.
  4. "A CAP reform?": verdict on substance; status quo plus flexibility and complexity.
  5. "Fairness and flexibility": who gets what; national à la carte choices.
  6. "Final ratification yet to come": next procedural steps.
- **How this post uses it:** same six moves for autumn 2026: (1) Council partial position 26 Oct, €141bn presidency compromise, 13 Nov protest; (2) Matthews' 2013 title quoted as the frame; (3) "What was agreed, and what was not"; (4) "How big is the cut?" plus "Two hats"; (5) "What a reform this is"; (6) "Still to come". Matthews quoted only for his title and the short phrase "rather unlikely"; none of his sentences reused.

## Claims
- Council partial negotiating position on post-2027 CAP, 26 Oct 2026; excludes financial and horizontal issues; Heydon "major milestone for the Irish Presidency"; NRPP single plan; two funds merged; DABIS; mandatory active farmer definition; higher DABIS for young farmers; degressivity and capping set later in MFF talks; final ringfenced amount agreed in overall MFF: Council press release (Consilium). Primary.
- Ireland proposed €141bn cuts to the next MFF (October); ~200 farmers by bus; Grand Canal near Convention Centre where EU leaders met informally, 13 Nov 2026; IFA "24% cut ... 24% reduction in payments"; Gorman quotes (Taoiseach "would not countenance a weakened CAP budget"; 75% of EU money via CAP); Minister Byrne "intended to bridge the many differences": TheJournal, 13 Nov 2026.
- €295.7bn ringfenced (CAP income support and fisheries) vs €387.8bn CAP 2021-27, current prices, -24% nominal; like-for-like income support reduction about 15% nominal; real terms larger: Matthews, capreform.eu, 19 July 2025. Commission headline "around €300 billion" incl. crisis measures (Council release); sources range €293.7bn to €300bn depending on what is included.
- Ringfence is a minimum; national top-ups "rather unlikely"; national split (Annex) not published at the time: Matthews, capreform.eu, 20 July 2025. Not confirmed whether national allocations have since been published; post says "has not been published". RE-CHECK before publication.
- Ireland CAP Strategic Plan 2023-27: €9.8bn total, €2.3bn Exchequer: gov.ie (DAFM press release). €7.5bn EU; Pillar I €5.97bn EU over five years (~€1.2bn/yr): Agriland / EU CAP Network summary. FY2024 Pillar 1 payments €1.2bn (secondary).
- Illustration: 15% of ~€1.2bn/yr = ~€180m/yr, nominal; labelled as illustration in the post.
- 2013: Irish presidency Jan-Jun 2013; MFF ceilings fixed by European Council Feb 2013 and unchanged in final agreement; CAP political agreement with Parliament 26-27 June 2013: Matthews posts "A triumph..." and "The CAP budget in the MFF agreement".
- MFF requires unanimity in the Council and European Parliament consent (Article 312 TFEU). Irish presidency ends 31 Dec 2026 (July-December 2026 rotation).

## Pre-publication check, 26 November 2026

Replaces the "RE-CHECK before publication" note on national allocations above.

- **National allocations: the draft was wrong.** The Commission announced per-country figures in September 2025; Ireland's CAP minimum is EUR 8.16bn in EU funds over 2028-2034 (proposal): Irish Farmers Journal, 30 Sept 2025. Price basis not stated there. The article is loose on coverage ("all farm and rural schemes", then the new area-based, coupled and small-farmer support), so the post does not compare it with Pillar I or give an Irish percentage cut. Matthews' July 2025 post (annex then empty) predates it. A primary Commission, gov.ie or Oireachtas text for the figure was NOT read: gov.ie and oireachtas.ie returned 403 to the fetch tool.
- **Irish presidency box (c. 10 Oct):** CAP minimum ring-fence unchanged from the Commission; overall MFF about 8% lower; agricultural crisis reserve EUR 630m a year against the Commission's 900m: Matthews, capreform.eu/the-irish-presidency-negotiating-box/.
- **Costa box (21 Nov):** CAP ring-fence unchanged; at least 10% of the global NRP amount, excluding CAP and fisheries minimums and the Social Climate Fund, guaranteed to CAP (usable for DABIS too); small extra for Ireland; EUR 300,000 capping mandatory, degressivity voluntary: Matthews, capreform.eu, 23 Nov 2026. Cross-checked on the unchanged CAP total with Irish Farmers Journal, 21 Nov, and Agence Europe, 23 Nov.
- **Summit 26-27 Nov:** under way; no agreement expected at it, December summit next: RTE, 26 Nov 2026 (updated 09:02). Outcome not known at publication.
- **No final deal**, so the core argument stands. No change to the Commission's EUR 295.7bn ring-fence found (sources round it differently, EUR 293-296bn, depending on what is included).
- **EUR 141bn:** TheJournal, 13 Nov.
- **Presidency end:** 31 December; RTE calls the December summit the presidency's final major act. consilium.europa.eu returned 403, so the Council pages were not re-read.
