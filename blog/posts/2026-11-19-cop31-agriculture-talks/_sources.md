# Claim ledger

Compiled 19 November 2026, while COP31 (Antalya, 9 to 20 November 2026) was still in session. Public sources only; excluded from the Quarto build. Re-check the COP31 cover decision before publication.

## Model piece (workflow Stage 4, 1990 to 2015 rule)

- **Model:** Doreen Stabinsky, "The 'deal' in Durban: What happened on agriculture at the climate talks?", IATP blog, 13 December 2011. https://www.iatp.org/blog/201112/deal-durban-what-happened-agriculture-climate-talks
- **Its structure, which this post borrows:**
  1. Opens by granting that agriculture both affects and is affected by climate change, then names the real difficulty: how the UNFCCC should handle it. Notes the text has barely moved for years.
  2. Asks outright why agriculture is so controversial.
  3. First answer: framing. Agriculture sat in a mitigation workstream while developing countries saw it as adaptation. Copenhagen history.
  4. Second answer: trade. Who insisted on what (Argentina, Brazil vs New Zealand, US). Cancún deadlock.
  5. Turns to the deeper question: why one side wanted a decision so badly (soil-carbon market methodologies).
  6. "So what finally happened?": the desired outcome against the actual text. Result: no work programme, only an exchange of views.
  7. What next: the agenda moves to other forums. Closing point: its drivers do not need a UNFCCC decision.
- **How this post uses it:** same seven moves, filled with 2026 evidence: (1) IPCC and FAO/UNDP numbers, Antalya outcome; (2) the question; (3) and (4) Stabinsky's 2011 account as history, then (4) trade returns, (3) scope returns as "food systems" vs "agriculture"; (5) what a decision is worth now (adaptation finance); (6) what Antalya produced; (7) initiatives outside the talks, ending on Addis Ababa. Stabinsky is cited openly for her 2011 facts. None of her sentences are reused.

## Numbers
- AFOLU about 22% of global net anthropogenic GHG emissions in 2019 (13 GtCO2-eq): IPCC AR6 WGIII Summary for Policymakers, B.2.2. Primary.
- 64 developing-country NAPs; agrifood systems need 54% of adaptation finance, receive 20% of adaptation funds; about 1% of total climate finance: FAO newsroom, COP30 Belém (November 2025), report "Agrifood Systems in National Adaptation Plans: An analysis" (FAO and UNDP). 54% and 20% read on FAO page. All three read on the FAO release ("just 1 percent of total climate finance").

## COP31 agriculture track
- SJWA created by decision 3/CP.27 (2022), four years, report back at COP31: FAO SJWA page. KJWA set up at COP23 in 2017: FAO page.
- SB closing: Co-Facilitator Claudia Heidecke (Germany) invited views on draft procedural conclusions; various parties disappointed; EU supported by Brazil proposed "friends of progress" group; farmers' constituency "lamented feeling left behind"; SBs adopted conclusions FCCC/SB/2026/L.5 containing a draft COP decision, later adopted by the COP: ENB daily report dated 14 Nov 2026. Record of negotiations (IISD), near-primary. UNFCCC PDF of L.5 could not be retrieved (URLs return HTML), so not linked.
- Decision moved to 2027, Bonn (June) or COP32 Addis Ababa: Devex, Elissa Miolene, 16 Nov 2026 (opening paragraphs; rest paywalled).
- "Text full of political red lines, including trade measures and adaptation finance"; Teresa Anderson (ActionAid) quote "railroaded the whole thing": Carbon Brief Cropped, 18 Nov 2026.
- "Food systems" absent from the draft cover text published Monday 16 Nov; oceans mentioned 21 times: Carbon Brief Cropped, 18 Nov 2026. The cover decision may still change; re-check.

## Trade and scope
- Draft cover decision, trade section: "serious concern" over unilateral climate-related trade-restrictive measures; calls on developed countries to phase them out by end-2027; presidency draft, not agreed: Dhaka Tribune (and Climate Watch). Secondary; verify against the final cover decision.
- Just transition talks (CMA contact group): African Group, Arab Group, AOSIS opposed "food systems", preferred "agriculture"; EU and EIG supported food systems approach; Arab Group and Nigeria highlighted negative impacts of unilateral trade measures: ENB daily report 16 Nov 2026. This is the just transition track, not the agriculture track itself; the post says so.
- EUDR scope (cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya, wood; cut-off 31 Dec 2020): existing EKO post, Regulation (EU) 2023/1115.
- "Several summits" pushing unilateral trade measures onto the agenda: BASIC agenda proposal at COP28 (UNFCCC document); China seeking a CBAM item for COP30 (S&P Global, Nov 2024).
- CBAM described generally ("prices the carbon in some imports"); no figures claimed.

## Outside the negotiations
- FAO turquoise nexus initiative; Ethiopia joined Alliance of Champions for Food Systems Transformation; 7 of 159 Global Methane Pledge countries reaffirmed 30% by 2030: Carbon Brief Cropped, 18 Nov 2026.
- COP32 Addis Ababa: Devex. "First African COP since Sharm el-Sheikh": COP27 Egypt 2022; COP28 UAE, COP29 Azerbaijan, COP30 Brazil, COP31 Türkiye.

## Interpretation (labelled as Elvis's reading in the post)
- "Food systems" vs "agriculture" as the 2011 scope dispute in new vocabulary.
- The track fails because trade, finance and food-emissions responsibility meet there; consensus lets one red line stop it.
- The post does not say which countries held the adaptation-finance red line (not reported).
